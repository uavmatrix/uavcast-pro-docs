# Stream to multiple ground stations

One drone, several screens: a pilot on Mission Planner, a payload operator on QGroundControl, a supervisor watching from the office. UAVcast-Pro sends telemetry and video to every destination you add, each with its own switches.

## How it works

Each entry under **Ground Control Stations** is one destination: an IPv4 address plus a telemetry port and a video port. UAVcast-Pro sends:

- MAVLink telemetry over UDP to **Telemetry Port** (default 14550) when **Enable Telemetry** is on,
- H.264 video over RTP/UDP to **Video Port** (default 5600) when **Enable Video** is on,
- nothing at all when **Enable Endpoint** is off.

Commands from every destination reach the flight controller, unless that destination is **Read Only**.

## 1. Add a destination per station

<Steps>

1. Open **Ground Control Stations** and press **Add Destination** once for each station.
2. Give each a **Name** you will recognize in the field, for example *Pilot – Mission Planner* and *Payload – QGC*.
3. Enter each computer's IPv4 address in **Destination IP or Host**. Over 4G, use VPN addresses.
4. Switch on **Enable Telemetry** and **Enable Video** where they are needed. A station that only needs telemetry saves bandwidth with video off.

</Steps>

## 2. Make observers read-only

For anyone who should watch but never fly, switch on **Read Only** on their destination. The station still receives live telemetry, but it can't change anything on the vehicle: commands, mode changes, arming, parameter writes, mission writes and RC overrides from it are blocked. {/* PENDING-RELEASE: read-only */} Ground station software may show warnings in this mode, because some of its requests (such as downloading parameters or the mission) go unanswered.

:::warning Two pilots, one drone
Without Read Only, every station can command the drone. Agree who flies, and make every other station read-only.
:::

## 3. Two stations on one computer

Two programs on one computer can't listen on the same port. Add two destinations with the same IP and different ports, for example:

| Destination | Telemetry Port | Video Port |
|---|---|---|
| Mission Planner | 14550 | 5600 |
| QGroundControl | 14551 | 5602 |

Then set the second program to listen on those ports (in QGroundControl, add a UDP comm link on 14551 and set the video UDP port to 5602). Leave a gap between video ports if you use [dual stream](./dual-camera-rc-switching.mdx), because the second camera goes to video port + 1.

## 4. Mind the uplink

Every video destination is a separate copy of the stream. Three video destinations at 1500 kbps need about 4.5 Mbit/s of uplink from the drone. Over 4G, send video only to the stations that need it, or lower the bitrate on **Camera → Camera settings**. **Dashboard → Network Traffic** shows what the modem actually sends.

## 5. Check that every station gets data

On **Dashboard**, the **Ground station** row shows *Sending to n · x packets/s*. The **Connection check** on **Flight Controller → Connection** lists each ground station output, and any destination left out because its address is invalid.

## With Fleet Manager

In Fleet Manager, ground stations join the organization's VPN and you connect them to drones with **Stream Bindings** on the **Network** page. Each additional drone bound to the same ground station gets its own block of ports. {/* PENDING-RELEASE: port-blocks */} See [Stream bindings](../fleet-manager/network/stream-bindings.mdx).
