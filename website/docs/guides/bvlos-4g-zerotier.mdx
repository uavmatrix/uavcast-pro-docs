# Fly beyond visual range over 4G with UAVnet

This guide takes a drone from the bench to a flight over the cellular network: telemetry and live video in QGroundControl or Mission Planner, wherever both ends have coverage. It uses UAVnet, UAVmatrix's hosted ZeroTier network, so neither side needs a public IP or port forwarding.

**Time:** about an hour. **You need:**

- A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 (or another [supported board](../reference/supported-hardware.mdx)) with UAVcast-Pro [installed](../get-started/installation.mdx) and the [license activated](../get-started/license-activation.mdx).
- An ArduPilot flight controller, a USB data cable or TELEM wiring.
- A USB 4G/LTE modem and a data SIM. Disable the SIM PIN in a phone first, it saves trouble.
- A camera (CSI or USB), if you want video.
- A laptop with QGroundControl or Mission Planner, and the [ZeroTier client](https://www.zerotier.com/download/).

:::warning Rules for beyond-visual-line-of-sight flight
Flying beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) needs an authorization in most countries. UAVcast-Pro gives you the link; the operational approval, the risk assessment and the fail-safes are your responsibility.
:::

## How it fits together

```text
Flight controller ──USB/UART── UAVcast-Pro ──4G──► internet ──► laptop
                                    │                               │
                                    └──── UAVnet (ZeroTier) VPN ────┘
                       telemetry: UDP 14550   video: UDP 5600
```

The drone sends MAVLink and H.264 video to your laptop's VPN address. The VPN makes the laptop reachable over 4G as if both were on the same local network.

## 1. Connect the flight controller

<Steps>

1. Plug the flight controller into the Pi over USB, or wire a TELEM port (TX→RX, RX→TX, GND→GND) and set `SERIALx_PROTOCOL = 2` on that port.
2. In UAVcast-Pro, open **Flight Controller → Connection**. Choose **USB** or **UART / Serial** under **Link type**, and leave **Port** and **Baud rate** on **Automatic**.
3. Wait for the status **Connected** and a vehicle in **Vehicle**. If it stays at **No heartbeat**, see [No MAVLink heartbeat](../troubleshooting/no-mavlink-heartbeat.mdx).

</Steps>

## 2. Set up the modem

<Steps>

1. Plug in the modem and open **Modem**.
2. Set **Enable cellular Modem?** to **Yes** and pick the **Modem type**: **HiLink** for modems that act as a USB network adapter (for example Huawei E3372h), **Traditional (PPP)** for the rest.
3. For a Traditional modem, leave **Access Point Name (Optional)** empty. UAVcast-Pro finds the APN for your SIM and remembers it.
4. Under **Modem Information**, wait for an operator and an IPv4 address.
5. Go to **Networks** and choose **Modem Priority (Outdoor Mode)**, so traffic leaves over the modem even when Wi-Fi is around.

</Steps>

Details: [Cellular modem](../uavcast-pro/connectivity/cellular-modem.mdx). If the modem connects but nothing loads: [Modem connected, no internet](../troubleshooting/modem-no-internet.mdx).

## 3. Create a UAVnet network

<Steps>

1. In UAVcast-Pro, open **Virtual Private Network** and click **Open UAVNet Controller**, or go to [uavnet.uavmatrix.com](https://uavnet.uavmatrix.com). Sign in with your UAVnet account (email and password), or create one on the sign-in page.
2. Create a network and copy its **Network ID** (16 characters).

</Steps>

## 4. Put the drone on the network

<Steps>

1. On the **Virtual Private Network** page, set **Enable VPN** to **Yes** and **Services** to **ZeroTier**.
2. Paste the **Network ID** and press **Join Network**.
3. In UAVnet, authorize the new member if it is not authorized yet. Under **Connected Networks** in UAVcast-Pro, the network's status changes from **ACCESS_DENIED** to `OK`.
4. Note the drone's IP address shown for the network under **Connected Networks**.

</Steps>

## 5. Put the laptop on the network

<Steps>

1. Install ZeroTier on the laptop and join the same Network ID: on Windows from the tray icon (**Join New Network**), on Linux with `sudo zerotier-cli join <network-id>`.
2. Authorize the laptop in UAVnet.
3. Note the laptop's ZeroTier address. On Windows, `ipconfig` shows it on the ZeroTier adapter.
4. Test: `ping <drone ZeroTier IP>` from the laptop. You can now open the drone's web interface at `http://<drone ZeroTier IP>/`.

</Steps>

## 6. Send telemetry and video to the laptop

<Steps>

1. In UAVcast-Pro, open **Ground Control Stations** and press **Add Destination**.
2. **Name**: for example *Field laptop*. **Destination IP or Host**: the laptop's **ZeroTier** address.
3. Keep **Telemetry Port** `14550` and **Video Port** `5600`.
4. Switch on **Enable Telemetry** and **Enable Video**. Both are off on a new destination.
5. On the laptop, allow incoming UDP 14550 and 5600 in the firewall (commands in [No video, check 3](../troubleshooting/no-video-in-gcs.mdx#check-3)).

</Steps>

## 7. Start the camera

<Steps>

1. Open **Camera → Camera settings**, set **Enable Camera?** to **Yes** and choose the camera in **Select Camera**.
2. For LTE, start with **Resolution** 1280×720, **Select FPS** 15–20 and **Bitrate:** around 1000–1500 kbps.
3. Settings save as you change them. Press **Start** if the camera isn't running yet.

</Steps>

## 8. Open the ground station

- **QGroundControl** connects to UDP 14550 by itself. For video, set **Source** to **UDP h.264 Video Stream** on port 5600 in the video settings.
- **Mission Planner**: choose **UDP**, press **Connect**, accept port 14550. Video shows in the HUD.

## 9. Test on 4G before you fly

<Steps>

1. Turn off the Pi's Wi-Fi, or take the drone away from it, so the only path is the modem.
2. Check the dashboard: **Cellular** connected, **VPN** connected, **Ground station** *Sending*, **Video** running.
3. Watch the link for 10 minutes on the ground, ideally at the take-off site. Look for video freezes and telemetry gaps.
4. On **Flight Controller → Connection**, check **GCS Failsafe Information**. If it shows **Warning - Failsafe Disabled**, enable the GCS failsafe on the flight controller (`FS_GCS_ENABL` on Plane, `FS_GCS_ENABLE` on Copter) and choose its action, so the drone acts on its own when the link drops.

</Steps>

:::tip Record every flight
Turn on **Record automatically when armed** under **Camera → Recordings → Settings**. You get the video and a telemetry log of every flight for later review: [Record flights and review them](./recording-flight-review.mdx).
:::
