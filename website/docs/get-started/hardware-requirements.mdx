# Hardware requirements

UAVcast-Pro runs on a 64-bit Linux companion computer connected to any MAVLink flight controller (ArduPilot or PX4). Add a camera for video and a 4G/LTE modem for flights beyond Wi-Fi range. A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with a Pi camera and a USB modem is the most common setup.

## Companion computer

UAVcast-Pro installs on any **64-bit** Linux board or PC with an **arm64** or **amd64 (x86-64)** CPU running Raspberry Pi OS, Debian or Ubuntu. 32-bit systems (`armv7l`, `i686`) are not supported.

| Board | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Raspberry Pi 5 | Supported | No hardware H.264 encoder. Video is encoded in software, so watch the **System load** indicator on the Camera page with high resolutions or two streams. |
| Raspberry Pi 4 | Supported, recommended | Good balance of CPU, power draw and price. 2 GB RAM or more. |
| Raspberry Pi 3 / 3B+ | Supported | Fine for telemetry and one 720p stream. Installation takes about 30 minutes. |
| Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W | Supported | Smallest and lightest. Installation takes about 30 minutes; keep video modest. |
| Raspberry Pi Compute Module 3+/4 | Supported | Same as the matching Pi model. |
| NVIDIA Jetson, Orange Pi, Banana Pi and other arm64 boards | Supported | Use the vendor's 64-bit Ubuntu or Debian image. UAVcast-Pro recognises these boards and suggests their serial ports for the flight controller. Use a USB camera (Raspberry Pi CSI cameras need a Pi). |
| x86-64 PC (Intel NUC, mini PC, laptop) | Supported | Uses the amd64 build. USB serial adapters show up as `/dev/ttyUSB*` or `/dev/ttyACM*`. |

Run `uname -m` on the board to check the architecture. You want `aarch64` or `x86_64`.

:::note Non-Raspberry Pi arm64 boards
Jetson, Orange Pi, Banana Pi and other arm64 boards that aren't a Raspberry Pi need {/* PENDING-RELEASE: non-pi-arm64 version */}the next UAVcast-Pro release or later. Older installers stop on them with *Device is not a Raspberry Pi*.
:::

### Storage

- **Minimum 500 MB free** on the root disk, or the installer stops. An 8 GB or larger card is the practical minimum; use 32 GB or more if you record video on the drone.
- Use a good A1/A2-rated microSD card, or boot from SSD. Recordings can also go to a USB drive or SSD (see [Recordings](../uavcast-pro/camera/recordings.mdx)).

:::note The license is tied to the storage device
UAVcast-Pro identifies a device by its CPU serial number plus the serial number of the boot disk (SD card or SSD). Moving to a new SD card counts as a new device. Unregister the old one first. See [Move your license to new hardware](./license-activation.mdx#move-to-new-hardware).
:::

### Power

The Raspberry Pi needs a stable **5.1 V** supply that can carry the Pi plus everything on its USB ports. A 4G modem can draw well over 500 mA when it transmits, and a USB camera adds more.

- Power the Pi from a dedicated 5 V BEC or regulator sized for the whole load. A flight controller's TELEM or USB port cannot power the Pi.
- If the modem browns out the Pi, power it from a powered USB hub or a separate 5 V supply.
- Check the **Power supply** row on the [Dashboard](../uavcast-pro/dashboard.mdx). *Voltage too low - use a stronger power supply* means the Pi is under-voltage right now. *Slowed down (heat or power)* means the CPU is throttling.

## Flight controller

UAVcast-Pro works with **any MAVLink flight controller**: ArduPilot (Copter, Plane, Rover, Sub) and PX4, on Pixhawk and Cube families, Matek, Holybro, CUAV, mRo and most other boards. MAVLink 2 is recommended.

Flight-mode names and the mode buttons (in the Flight Map and in Fleet Manager) use ArduPilot's modes for Copter, Plane, Rover and Sub. On a PX4 flight controller, telemetry, video, routing to ground stations, the map and the HUD work the same way; use your ground station to change PX4 flight modes.

Connect it in one of two ways:

| Link | Wiring | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **USB** | USB cable from the flight controller's USB port to the companion computer. | Easiest. No baud rate to set. Use a short, good-quality data cable. |
| **UART / Serial** | TELEM port TX → computer RX, RX → computer TX, GND → GND. Or a USB-serial (FTDI) cable. | When the USB port is used for something else, or for a cleaner install. Enable MAVLink on that port: `SERIALx_PROTOCOL = 2` (MAVLink2) on ArduPilot, `MAV_x_CONFIG` on PX4. |

**Raspberry Pi only:** the GPIO UART is `/dev/serial0` (GPIO 14 TX, GPIO 15 RX). The installer frees it for the flight controller: it disables the serial login console and, on Pi 3, 4 and Zero 2 W, moves Bluetooth off the UART. Never connect the flight controller's 5 V pin to the Pi when both have their own supply.

On other boards, use the board's UART (for example `ttyTHS*` on Jetson, `ttyS*` on Orange Pi) or a USB-serial adapter.

| Flight controller TELEM port | Raspberry Pi GPIO header |
|---|---|
| TX | GPIO 15 (RXD), pin 10 |
| RX | GPIO 14 (TXD), pin 8 |
| GND | GND, for example pin 6 |

TX always goes to RX. Leave the TELEM port's 5 V pin unconnected.

UAVcast-Pro finds the port and baud rate automatically. See [Connect the flight controller](../uavcast-pro/flight-controller/index.mdx).

## Cameras

| Camera | Notes |
|---|---|
| Raspberry Pi camera modules (v1, v2, v3, HQ, Global Shutter) and other libcamera CSI cameras | Best latency. Raspberry Pi 5 and Compute Modules can run two CSI cameras. |
| USB (UVC) webcams such as Logitech C920 or C615 | Plug and play. Picked up as `/dev/video*`. |
| HDMI sources (GoPro, camera gimbals) | Through an HDMI-to-CSI bridge (shows up as a CSI camera) or a USB HDMI capture stick (shows up as a USB camera). |
| IP cameras and anything else GStreamer can read | Through a [custom pipeline](../uavcast-pro/camera/custom-pipeline.mdx). |

Two cameras can run at the same time, either switched from an RC channel or streamed together. See [Two cameras and RC switching](../uavcast-pro/camera/multi-camera.mdx). For audio, any USB microphone or a webcam with a built-in mic works. See [Audio](../uavcast-pro/camera/audio.mdx).

## 4G/LTE modems

UAVcast-Pro supports two kinds of USB modem:

| Type | How it works | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **HiLink** | The modem is a small router. It shows up as a network card (usually `eth1` or `usb0`) and connects by itself. Nothing to configure in UAVcast-Pro. | Huawei E3372**h** (HiLink firmware), E8372, most "LTE USB dongle with web UI" sticks |
| **Traditional (PPP)** | Managed by ModemManager and NetworkManager. UAVcast-Pro connects it, finds the APN and reconnects after drops. | Huawei E3372**s** (stick firmware), Quectel EC25 / EG25 / EM06 and Sierra Wireless modules in a USB adapter |

Any modem on the [ModemManager supported devices list](https://www.freedesktop.org/wiki/Software/ModemManager/SupportedDevices/) should work as a Traditional modem. Use a SIM with a data plan for your region, and check whether your operator blocks inbound traffic (a VPN works around this). See [Cellular modem](../uavcast-pro/connectivity/cellular-modem.mdx).

## Ground station computer

Any computer that runs Mission Planner, QGroundControl or another MAVLink ground station. It receives telemetry on UDP 14550 and video on UDP 5600 by default. For video in Mission Planner you need GStreamer installed; QGroundControl has it built in.
